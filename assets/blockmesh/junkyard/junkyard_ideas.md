# Junkyard props and playable layout ideas

## Best next large landmarks

- Car crusher: a low loading bed, heavy hinged crushing lid, hydraulic supports
  and a pile of compressed car blocks. A strong focal point beside the crane.
- Scrap baler: a box-shaped press that turns loose metal into compact cubes.
- Workshop: corrugated shed, work bay, tool bench, shelving and roof access.
- Tipped bus: a large obstacle with space to travel around or through its shell.
- Car racks: two-level frames holding cars, creating overhead shapes and lanes.
- Weighbridge and small gate office near the entrance.
- Shipping containers: closed, open-ended, or damaged; useful lane boundaries.
- Stationary conveyor and sorting hopper for an industrial processing corner.

## Railway salvage corner

Yes, railway pieces fit the concept if part of the yard handles rail salvage.
Start with a few sections rather than a whole train so the setting stays readable.

- Short rail lengths, sleepers and stacked track panels.
- Detached railway wheelsets: two wheels and their shared axle.
- A bogie: the wheel-and-suspension frame beneath a railway carriage.
- A cut-down freight wagon body or one wagon on a short dead-end track.
- A damaged passenger carriage section, with windows or a cut opening.
- Couplers, buffers, brake parts and steel side panels as smaller scrap.
- Optional locomotive shell as a very large landmark if the arena has room.

Keep rail salvage in one corner with a short track entering through the boundary;
that gives it a visual reason to be there. These are design suggestions, not new
models included in this pack.

## Medium obstacles and reusable heaps

- Crushed-car blocks in low rows or stepped stacks.
- Truck chassis, stripped van shell, overturned trailer and detached truck cab.
- Axle piles, engine heaps, gearbox piles and radiator stacks.
- Sheet-metal bundles, corrugated sheets and folded vehicle panels.
- Steel I-beams, pipe bundles, wire spools and cable reels.
- Tire heaps, oversized tractor tires and rims without tires.
- Pallet stacks, scrap cages, open skips and small utility cabinets.
- Barrels, tanks, old appliances and metal lockers.
- Concrete barriers, broken slabs and rubble banks.

## Small dressing for later

- Loose wheels, batteries, exhausts, grilles, doors and bumpers.
- Toolboxes, jacks, welding cylinders, work lights and carts.
- Fence posts, wire panels, warning signs and numbered sorting bays.
- Weeds, shrubs, stones, dirt banks and tire-track decals.

## Placement for the current arena

1. Establish an entrance and a broad loop around the centre.
2. Use workshop, crusher, bus and rail salvage as distinct destinations.
3. Divide the empty floor into clusters with the large scrap and tire heaps;
   keep gaps between clusters so the player can choose routes.
4. Place lower heaps near corners and crossings so the player can see ahead.
5. Put long piles against walls or use them to guide movement toward landmarks.
6. Leave turning room around the crane and crusher loading areas.
7. Test route widths with the actual robot, including its full leg span, before
   filling gaps with decorative scrap. Keep one easy route and optional tighter
   shortcuts. Add collision shapes separately; these OBJ props have none.

The screenshot shows a lot of open floor. A few major landmarks and grouped
obstacles will make it playable more effectively than evenly scattering clutter.
